# WP13 notes — the remote usability test

Material for the course report (usability chapter); the research core (§1–§10) is unchanged and
`sentinel reproduce --check` stays 117 of 117.

**Method.** The test of `docs/usability/protocol-pl.md` runs remotely and unmoderated: two or
three people who lead projects get a written instruction in Polish, the CSV of seven tasks and a
list of five people, open the public demo in a private window and do the five tasks in one tab,
then send an answer card (clock times per task, answers to tasks 2, 4 and 5, ease 1–5, the ten
SUS items of Brooke 1996). Without a moderator there is no thinking aloud (Ericsson and Simon
1993) and no observed path; the app's own records replace the observation. Each tester names the
project with a code (P1, P2, P3), so the owner finds the sandbox without any identifier of the
person.

**What the app records.** A sandbox's event log already dates the project, the plan, each status
report with its content, each recommendation, decision and simulator advance; the job table dates
the simulation's end. What it lacked is the start (the sandbox's first request), the pages opened
and the forms refused, so the demo now logs each sandbox's requests with the route's template,
the status and an HTMX flag, but no address, query or form content (ADR-0030); the rows go with
the sandbox after 24 hours. `scripts/usability_logs.py` derives the task durations, each from
the end of the one before: task 1 from the first request to the plan, task 3 to the saved report
(task 2, reading the overview, leaves no trace and is inside it; the card's clock times split
it), task 4 to the approval or rejection, task 5 to the end of the simulation. It counts the
pages opened (not the fragments a page loads) and the refused form submissions (422: an error or
a question to confirm) in each interval, the closest measure the protocol's error count has
without an observer. The forecast the overview showed after the plan is not stored; the export
computes it again from the log, which is exact because a forecast is deterministic for the
project's state (spec §11.4).

**Expected values (1.4.1, demo mode, any test day).** After the plan: 69 %, most likely finish
Wed 2 Dec 2026, bad case Fri 11 Dec (11 November a holiday). The status report of 4 November
saves 15 % as 17 % (whole days, 1 of 6). The recommendation: Celina Wiśniewska takes over the
absent Bartosz Kowalski's share of "Baza danych i API", 39 % → 67 %, cost 100 % → 102 %. The
small simulation (j3026_7, typical disruptions, seed fixed): Sentinel 8 working days late, no
reaction 18, Sentinel 10 working days earlier at 103 % against 102 % of the budget; only its
calendar dates move with the test day. These are asserted by `tests/test_ui_usability.py`.

**Baseline.** The script's run of the same steps in demo mode (no reading or thinking, the
server's work included; `baseline-timing.json`): 9 clicks and 4 fields in 2.7 s, 0.2 s, 6 clicks
and 5 fields in 1.7 s, 2 clicks in 2.8 s, 3 clicks in 58.1 s (two 60-day steps of the demo).

**Scoring and limits.** SUS: odd items answer − 1, even items 5 − answer, sum × 2.5; with 3
people report the mean and range only (Sauro and Lewis 2016; average about 68). Two or three
testers find a minority of the problems five would (Nielsen and Landauer 1993); clock times
written by the tester are coarse (minutes), hence the log's durations; a tester who uses two
browsers has two sandboxes, both exported.
