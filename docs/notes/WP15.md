# WP15 notes — interface v2, part 1 (material for the article and the course report)

**Design basis.** The v2 interface implements `docs/design/v2-spec.md`, agreed with the owner in a
design workshop on 2026-11-07. Its principles come from information-dashboard design (Few 2006:
one screen readable at a glance, details one click away), pre-attentive processing (Ware 2012;
Healey & Enns 2012: status by colour, size and position first, text confirms), working-memory
limits (Cowan 2001: about four blocks above the fold), choice and pointing time (Hick 1952; Fitts
1954: few, large decision buttons), natural frequencies and icon arrays for risk (Gigerenzer &
Hoffrage 1995; Galesic et al. 2009: "39 na 100" with a 10 × 10 dot grid), and contrastive
explanation (Miller 2019: why this option and not the best alternative, in two sentences).

**Human in the loop.** Every change to a live project, a pure re-ordering of the plan included,
happens only after the manager approves it: v2 deliberates at autonomy `Λ = 0` (spec §11.8), the
strictest level of the permission gate, while the research configurations keep theirs. This
follows the levels-of-automation view (Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens 2000) for a decision with
consequences for people, and the oversight duty of the EU AI Act (Art. 14). A decision is never
applied by silence; it has a validity date and a cost of delay instead.

**Validity and cost of delay.** For the action a decision waits on, the chance of the deadline is
evaluated with the action approved `δ = 0, 1, …` working days later on the forecast's 200 worlds
(common random numbers), until it gains less than `ε_D = 0.02` over no change or `δ = 10`. The
showcase's substitution: 57, 51.5, 46, 44, 38 in 100 for `δ = 0…4`, against 39 without it, so it
is valid for three more working days (Thu 19 Nov) and each day of waiting costs about 5 in 100.
`p(0)` equals the card's figure exactly (the evaluator applies the action on day `t` as approval
does), which is what makes the cost of delay consistent with the promise on the card.

**Correction.** The manager can change the one parameter the model evaluates (another substitute
of the same role, another temporary person's role, number and window, another optional task);
the corrected action is evaluated on the same worlds as the comparison and becomes the choice,
so the promise-equals-result guarantee of WP12 holds for it too (tested).

**Numbers.** States: on track from 80 in 100 (`θ_D`, P80), at risk from 50, late without changes
below 50, where the most likely finish is after the deadline. The verdict's days are working days
between the median finish and the deadline. The "Zapas czasu" chart is a buffer-consumption
(fever) chart (Goldratt 1997; Leach 2014): x the share of work done in crew-member days, y the
share of the plan's reserve `D − C⁰` used by the median finish; zones split by the lines
`y = 2x/3` and `y = 1/3 + 2x/3`, a third each at half the work. The showcase reads 22 % of the work
with 40 % of the reserve used after the first report (amber) and 29 % with 120 % after the second
(red), the drop from 92 to 39 in 100.

**No per-person questions.** The wizard asks nothing about how often anyone is away. One
team-wide rate comes from ZUS, *Absencja chorobowa w 2025 roku* (14.76 days of sickness absence
per insured person, 4.0 % of days): `λ_A = 0.014` per person and working day with `E[L] = 3`
(ADR-0034). The team view of WP16 will show workload, never performance (Goodhart; Edmondson
1999; AI Act Annex III point 4).

**Voice.** A live grey preview while dictating: the recording so far goes to Groq's Whisper about
every 3 s (one request in flight), outside the demo's AI cap; ✓ writes out the whole recording
once more; nothing is stored (ADR-0031 update).
