# WP14 notes — a check-in message read as a whole report; voice input

**The failure.** In the owner's rehearsal of the usability test (1.4.1, gpt-oss-120b), a report of
three facts ("„Warsztat z klientem” skończył się wczoraj…", "„Baza danych i API” trwa, zrobiono
około 15 %", "Bartosz Kowalski od jutra jest nieobecny i wraca w poniedziałek 16 listopada")
gave one item, wrong in both dates: the application reused the research parser of spec section
10.2, which asks for working-day numbers, so the model counted days across the 11 November
holiday, and progress and finishes were not among its event types.

**The reader** (spec v2.3.0, section 11.3; ADR-0032). A separate schema and prompt for the
application: items of the check-in form (done, progress with share and start, absence with
since/until/back, more work, new task), each with its clause number. The division of labour is
the point: the model names, the code computes. Each date is `{"day": "YYYY-MM-DD", "words": …}`;
the words are read by a small Polish/English date grammar (weekdays and months in all cases,
today/tomorrow/yesterday, named days such as Wigilia, the end of a week or month, "next"/"last"
week) that yields the set of dates they allow, in the direction of the field (a return looks
forward, a finish backward). The model's date is kept only inside that set; a single allowed date
replaces it. Contradictions (a weekday that is not its date's), a day that is not a working day
and the two Mondays "od poniedziałku" can mean in the present tense become questions with the
candidate reports as choices. "Do <date>" is through that date (return on the next working day),
"wraca <date>" is that date, "od jutra" is the next working day after the report.

**Privacy by construction.** With `store_prompts = false` a valid answer is cached whole, so any
free text in it could carry a reason for an absence. The words of a date must therefore consist
of a closed vocabulary of date words (about 200 forms) and occur in the message (by meaning, so
"do poniedziałku" matches "poniedziałek"); any other word is a domain issue, the cache keeps only
the issue list, and a repair round asks again. Task words are checked against the project's task
names (a word matches a form of a name word: equal, or a common beginning of at least three
letters and all but the last three of the shorter word). A whitelist cannot miss an unlisted
reason, as the research parser's blacklist of English reason words could.

**Evaluation** (`docs/qa/ai/messages-pl.jsonl`, 30 reports, 43 expected items; `scripts/
messages_eval.py`). Exact share = exact items / (expected items + items the review added).

| Reader | Model | Exact | Wrong dates shown without a question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.4.1 (research parser + aids) | gpt-oss-120b | 7 / 43 (16.3 %) | 15 |
| 1.5.0 | gpt-oss-120b | 43 / 43 (100 %) | 0 |
| 1.4.1, first 10 | qwen2.5:7b | 1 / 13 + 2 added (6.7 %) | 6 |
| 1.5.0, first 10 | qwen2.5:7b | 7 / 13 + 1 added (50 %) | 0 (2 readings failed) |

The safety property held for the small model too: every date it got wrong was asked about or
not shown. The first 1.5.0 run scored 40/43 against the first expectations; its three misses
showed that "od poniedziałku" with a present-tense verb is genuinely ambiguous in Polish (the
model took the past Monday, plausible for a sick leave), so four expectations became questions.

**Voice** (ADR-0031). MediaRecorder at 32 kbit/s, at most 90 s; the raw body to the server, Groq's
`whisper-large-v3-turbo` with the team's and tasks' names as its prompt. The owner's message spoken
by Windows' Polish voice (15 s) came back in 0.46 s nearly verbatim but without full stops, so the
reader got one clause with three facts and still gave the three items (reading 1.9–2.5 s).
Nothing of a recording is stored; a local Whisper is the documented option for audio that must
stay inside (about real time on the 4-core server).
