In this application the message is also given as numbered clauses, and the answer has two more
fields besides "events":

- "unparsed": the numbers of the clauses that you did not turn into an event or a question, for
  example a clause about a meeting, praise or a question, or one that you could not read. Use []
  when every clause became an event or a question.
- "questions": what you cannot decide alone, each with the number of its clause:
  - {"kind": "unknown_person", "clause": n, "return_day": r}: the clause says that somebody is
    absent, but nobody in the roster has that name. Do not pick a worker for them; r is the
    return day as for an absence, or null.
  - {"kind": "ambiguous_task", "clause": n, "candidates": [j, ...], "extra_days": d}: the clause
    says that a task takes longer or more work, but its words fit two or more activities, for
    example two testing tasks. List the ids of all of them; d is the extra working days, or give
    "factor" instead of "extra_days".
  An event and a question never share a clause. Never copy words of the message into the answer.

Example: clauses [1] "Marek is on leave until Wednesday" and [2] "testing will take 2 days
longer", with no Marek in the roster and the activities 7 "Integration tests" and 9 "Acceptance
tests": {"events": [], "unparsed": [], "questions": [{"kind": "unknown_person", "clause": 1,
"return_day": 13}, {"kind": "ambiguous_task", "clause": 2, "candidates": [7, 9], "extra_days":
2}]}.
